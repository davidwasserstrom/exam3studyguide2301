Chapter 25: Economic Growth

25.1 Basics of Economic Growth

  • Real GDP growth vs. real GDP per person; labor quantity and productivity.
  • Calculate growth: (new GDP − old GDP) ÷ old GDP × 100.
  • Rule of 70: doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ annual growth rate (%).

25.2 Labor Productivity Growth

  • Labor productivity = real GDP ÷ aggregate hours worked.
  • Physical capital vs. consumer goods and financial capital; capital accumulation raises productivity.

25.3 Causes and Effects of Growth

  • Classical theory: population pressure, scarce resources, subsistence.
  • New growth theory: reusable knowledge, innovation, incentives, sustained growth.

25.4 Achieving Faster Growth

  • Economic freedom, property rights (including patents), markets and price signals.
  • Saving, investment, human capital, and differences in countries’ growth rates.

Chapter 26: Finance, Saving, and Investment

26.1 Financial Markets and Institutions

  • Financial vs. physical capital; stocks (ownership), bonds (promised payments), loans.
  • Financial intermediaries borrow and lend; bond prices and yields move inversely.

26.2 Loanable Funds Market

  • Sources: private saving, government surpluses, foreign borrowing.
  • Uses: business investment, government deficits, international lending/investment.
  • Saving supply and borrowing demand determine the real interest rate.

26.3 Government in Loanable Funds

  • Deficits raise borrowing demand and can raise real rates and crowd out investment.
  • Surpluses generally have the opposite effect, other things equal.

Chapter 27: The Monetary System

27.1 What Is Money?

  • Functions: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value.
  • Barter and double coincidence of wants; currency vs. cards.
  • Identify and calculate M1 and M2 from deposit and currency data.

27.2 Banking System

  • Bank assets (reserves, loans, securities) vs. liabilities (deposits).
  • Reserves = vault cash + Fed balances; excess = actual − desired reserves.
  • Reserve ratio = reserves ÷ deposits; money multiplier = (1 + c) ÷ (r + c), where c is currency drain/deposits and r is desired reserves/deposits.
  • Bank lending, deposit creation, balance sheets and reserve changes.

27.3 Federal Reserve System

  • Central bank, Board of Governors, FOMC, federal funds rate and discount rate.
  • Identify Fed monetary policy tools and FOMC’s policymaking role.

27.8 Regulating the Quantity of Money

  • Open market purchase → higher reserves; sale → lower reserves.
  • Trace reserves → lending → deposits → money quantity in the exam’s simplified model.

Chapter 28: Money, Interest, and Inflation

28.1 Money and Interest Rates

  • Money demand means money people choose to hold; opportunity cost = forgone interest.
  • Money vs. bonds; inverse bond price–yield relationship.
  • Short-run interest-rate response to money supply shifts; long-run price level and inflation effects.

28.3 Cost of Inflation

  • Purchasing power, inflation tax on holding money, misallocation and uncertainty.
  • Shoe-leather, confusion and uncertainty costs.
  • Hyperinflation: Germany 1923 vs. Venezuela 2016; monetary financing of government spending and social disruption.
